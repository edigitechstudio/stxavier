Yes — in most popular destinations international students can work part-time during term and full-time during official breaks, but every country limits the hours and the rules change often. Part-time income helps with living costs; it should not be your plan for paying tuition.

## How many hours can international students work?

Limits are set by each country's immigration rules and are printed on your visa conditions. Typical patterns are:

| Destination | Typical term-time rule |
|---|---|
| United Kingdom | Weekly hour limit for degree-level students |
| Australia | Fortnightly hour limit during term |
| Canada | Weekly off-campus hour limit during academic sessions |
| United States | On-campus work only during term (with exceptions such as CPT/OPT) |
| New Zealand | Weekly hour limit for eligible courses |
| Japan | Weekly limit with a work permission stamp |

Check the exact current limits on each [country guide](/destinations) and the official immigration website before you rely on them.

## Can part-time work pay for my studies?

Part-time earnings can cover some living costs, but visas require you to show you can pay tuition and living costs **before** you travel. Use our [cost calculator](/tools/cost-calculator) to plan your budget realistically.

## What happens if I work more than allowed?

Breaking work conditions can lead to visa cancellation and affect future applications. Always keep within the hours on your visa.
